Backup · B4 Questions I expect

Six questions, short answers.

“CXA Ops Center and Agent Builder already exist — what is new?”
The platform is the base, explicitly labeled as reused. New is the FS email vertical: regulated-intent detection at ingestion, assurance-tiered delegation with in-thread step-up, the dispute-intake agent the catalog does not have, email attack-surface defense, and a compliance-signable dossier.
“Glia already guarantees no hallucination and no injection impact.”
They govern what the AI says, topic by topic, and the guarantee covers Strict mode. We govern what it says, does and to whom — segment-level control inside one reply, plus an action-risk × identity-assurance matrix that governs writes, not wording.
“Is 25–35% VARR realistic?”
It is labeled an assumption. The anchor is Talkdesk's own 2026 finding: 38% of leading organizations already resolve over 40% of contacts — all-channel, not email. Step one of the pilot backtests on the customer's own 90 days of mail.
“How do you establish identity over email at all?”
We do not pretend to. SPF, DKIM and DMARC prove the domain was not forged, not that the sender holds the account — so identity is a four-level assurance rating recomputed on every arrival, with a single-use in-thread step-up link.
“Why not go after the big banks first?”
They self-build and Sierra owns that narrative. Mid-size banks and credit unions buy off the shelf, keep public mailboxes, sit under Reg E, and cluster exactly where the installed base and core integrations already are — 262 institutions.
“Cannibalizing seats — does the vendor lose?”
On assumption-based math: 32 seats × Elite $165/seat/month × 12 ≈ $63k of license a year — platform revenue, not bank cost. The same volume at $1.50 per resolution earns ~$268k. Net-additive for us, ~$2.68M a year for the bank. If we do not build it, that revenue goes to Glia, Sierra or Salesforce.
B4

Backup · B5 Four more questions I expect

Four more questions, short answers.

“Our CRM already holds the case — who is the system of record?”
The one that holds it today. We are the mail layer in front of it: detection, identity, the clock and the dossier, written back over the connectors CXA already ships. One case id, one owner, one audit trail — the only state we keep is the bank's own policy pack.
“What does one email cost in inference, against $1.50 a resolution?”
Single-digit cents, not dollars (assumption) — classification, retrieval, one drafting call and the claim check, per resolved thread. The margin that matters is not the token bill: the inference sits on license revenue we already collect, so it is cost, not the whole price.
“What is the team, and how much do you lean on CXA, Agent Builder, Automation Flows and connectors?”
One vertical team — product, engineering, a compliance lead, shared eval — on top of the platform. Everything horizontal is reused and labeled as reused: runtime, bot builder, automation, connectors. What we own is the vertical layer alone: intent detection, assurance tiers, the clock, the dossier.
“The window is 12–18 months. What holds it after that?”
Not a data flywheel — B11 rules out cross-customer training. What holds is the policy pack the bank wrote, the clock engine that turns a promise into a deadline, a dossier a regulator can be handed, and 262 institutions already on the voice contract. Leaving means re-writing their own limits somewhere else.

Four more, because these are the four that decide the follow-up: system of record, unit economics, the team, and what is left after the window.

Backup · B18 The seven rules every feature passes

The AI proposes. The rule engine decides.

RuleWhat it means in practice
1 · Never in the decision pathThe LLM never sits inside a decision. It advises — drafting, explaining, summarising, suggesting wording — it never judges; the rule engine decides.
2 · The ratchetTightening is immediate and time-boxed: a same-day change may only tighten, and must carry an expiry. Any loosening runs backtest → shadow → dual sign. Policy content changes are dual-signed either way.
3 · Predictions change wording, never outcomesCustomer value and churn never enter a decision — otherwise “the squeaky wheel gets the grease”, which is an ECOA and UDAAP problem. A consequence preview may reword a letter; it may not re-decide it.
4 · ROI first, evidence decidesROI decides who we build for first. Evidence decides whether it can be built at all — 90 days of samples, four proofs, zero critical misses.
5 · New things are born in shadowHumans set R and I levels. Candidates start shadow-classified and graduate normally. R3 never enters email; R4 stays L0 permanently.
6 · Proactive outreach is account facts onlyLocked templates plus slots, frequency caps, opt-out (statutory notices excepted), on-file addresses only, no marketing, no links asking for information or credentials. A decision not to send is logged too.
7 · Fairness is a required answerEvery nomination and policy diff carries cohort impact across language / 62+ / LMI tract / channel / stated vulnerability. Cohort data is monitored only — no protected attributes collected. The engine is deterministic, so unfairness comes from its surroundings: identification error, step-up drop-off, or approver variance at L2.

Why these seven: they are the difference between a demo and something a bank can put in front of an examiner. Each one is enforced in the prototype, not asserted on a slide — the engine refuses the action, the config layer has no switch, or the metric blocks the graduation.
