00Document Information & Summary

What This Document Is

This PRD defines the target users, scope, functional requirements, acceptance criteria, release cadence, and non-functional requirements for Email Autopilot for Financial Services (hereinafter "the Product"). The product is positioned as an FS (Financial Services) email vertical layer built on the existing CXA (Customer Experience Automation) platform: it does not rebuild the contact-center platform or the agent runtime; instead it adds FS-email-specific regulated-intent detection, statutory clocks, assurance-tiered delegation, dispute intake, and email attack-surface protection.

01Background & Problem

1.1 How Email Really Works in Financial-Service Support

Mail sent to a public mailbox (e.g. memberservices@larkspurbank.example) is ingested by the service platform as work item / ticket / case, routed by skill in the universal queue; human agents work it in the agent workspace, not in Outlook. This product is not a "help individuals write email" Copilot in Outlook; it is a digital employee running behind the universal queue that can work cases across days and systems.

The email channel at US financial institutions has two structural realities Analysis (no public statistics on exact ratios; per the fictional customer profile):

  • Large banks generally offer no public support mailbox; account matters go through logged-in, authenticated secure message inside online banking / apps; mid-size banks and credit unions generally keep public mailboxes, but their standard reply on account detail is "we can't discuss this over email for security reasons, please call…". That "please call us" is the status-quo baseline: one email becomes two contacts (email + phone) — a double loss on experience and cost.
  • Email authentication ≠ identity authentication: SPF/DKIM/DMARC only prove the sending domain wasn't spoofed — not that the sender is the account holder (the mailbox may be stolen). The FFIEC 2021 authentication guidance stresses layered security and multi-factor authentication — email itself is not an authentication factor, only one identity-assurance signal.

1.3 Market Timing

2.2 Design Principles

  1. We automate the work, not the decision. Automate procedural labor, never consequential adjudication.
  2. Autonomy is earned, not assumed. Restricted by default; autonomy is earned intent-by-intent through "graduation", with automatic downgrade on drift.
  3. Facts from systems, words from AI, rules from compliance. Amounts/dates/deadlines are filled only from system slots; regulatory disclosures are locked templates; AI handles understanding, organization, and empathy.
  4. The clock starts at the inbox. Regulated intents are detected and clocked at intake — even when buried as half a sentence in a multi-intent email.
  5. Security comes not from clever detectors but from dangerous actions being unreachable. Defense in depth + tool-signature-layer allowlists; R3 action combinations have no callable function on the email channel at all.
  6. Degrade, don't halt. On low confidence or guardrail failure, cases reroute to humans automatically — a case must never stall mid-flight or die in a queue.
  7. Building on CXA. Everything the platform already has (observability, evaluation, orchestration, connectors, workspace, fraud prevention) is reused and explicitly labeled — platform capabilities are never repackaged as new inventions.
  8. AI's wow space is design-time: who writes rules, who nominates delegation, who discovers new intents, who sees population anomalies. Three operating lines: AI nominates, evidence argues, humans grant; humans sign the diff, not every email (approving 1,000 similar emails a day is rubber-stamping; signing 3 evidence-backed diffs a week is not); every output of the new capabilities is a backtestable, signable, rollback-able configuration diff flowing through existing gates — no parallel governance.

06Core Concept Model

6.1 Email Thread Is the Case (Thread = Case)

An email's lifecycle inside the platform is fixed at 9 steps; every function of this product hangs off these 9 steps:

1
Ingest
Public mailboxes / secure message enter the universal queue; the server arrival timestamp is the sole start of the statutory clock (not when an agent opens it). Replies in the same thread auto-merge into one case; forwards, aliases, and +tag addresses are normalized.
2
Triage & intent
Multi-intent splitting (one email may mix inquiry + dispute + complaint); high-recall regulated-intent detection; language / vulnerability / fraud signals run in parallel.
3
Identity assurance
Emits I0–I3: I0 unmatchable to an account / I1 domain match only / I2 on-file mailbox match with passing SPF/DKIM/DMARC and no fraud signalsv0.2 Revised / I3 passed step-up (e.g. secure-message session, single-use link verification).
4
Policy evaluation
Deterministic questions (refund eligibility, investigation windows, material lists) are evaluated by the rules/flow engine; the LLM never decides policy outcomes — it only maps natural language into evaluation inputs.
5
Autonomy decision
Decided by the L0–L3 × R0–R4 × I0–I3 three-dimensional matrix: auto-resolve / draft-for-approval / intake-and-receipt-only / direct handoff / quarantine (see 6.2).
6
Action & orchestration
Core-system reads, file building, clock-start, and receipts run through Autopilot + Automation Flows; tool calls are authorized at the signature-layer allowlist; all writes are idempotent and compensable (saga).
7
Human checkpoint
L1 agents edit then send, L2 supervisors one-click approvev0.2 Revised, dispute adjudication, fraud confirmation; cases return automatically to Autopilot after human handling (notify customer, close, archive).
8
Customer response
Three-part assembly: regulatory disclosures and figures come from templates/slots (never through the model); explanation and empathy come from controlled generation; in-thread step-up link; AI identity disclosure; one-click handoff.

Autonomy is not a switch but a function of three dimensions: delegation tier L (where humans sit in the loop) × action risk R (reversibility and impact) × identity assurance I.

6.5 Division of Labor Between Autopilot and Automation Flows

  • Autopilot (LLM agent): understands mail, splits intents, judges routing, generates explanatory text, performs reads and low-risk writes inside authorized tools, and keeps conversational continuity with customers. It holds no multi-day state and never directly orchestrates multi-system writes.

  • Boundary rule: LLM output must never directly become write-operation parameters; write parameters come from slot extraction + policy evaluation + allowlist validation. Flows may call models for classification/summarization, but models must never rewrite the flow graph itself.

Illustrative: 186 statement_copy_request emails in 90 days, with agents following the same 4 steps at 91% consistency (identity I2 → retrieve → deliver via secure channel → close); AI provides volume trend, step consistency, cohort distribution, a suggested R/I level (R1/I2, set by a human), existing-tool mapping, and a locked-template draft. Proves the intent list is not hard-coded — it grows out of the mail.

Acceptance criteria

  • AC1: a candidate intent must include volume (90-day count and trend), human handling-step consistency, average handling time, cohort distribution, and mappable existing tools/data sources; missing any item keeps it out of the candidate area.
  • AC2: candidates are born in shadow (shadow classification, no intervention); after a human sets the R/I level they follow the normal §6.3 graduation path — AI may never create intents, wire tools, or request delegation itself.
  • AC3: R3/R4-nature clusters (legal documents subpoena/levy/garnishment) generate reports and routing to specialist groups only and are never auto-turned into intents (connects FR-10.5 and the §11.6 P2 long tail).
Depends on: FR-2.1, FR-9.2, FR-12.1; shares the FR-7.1 new-intent clustering signal. Non-goals: no auto-launch, no auto-connector wiring. Priority reading: under this document's P definitions (P0 = Wave 1 pilot, P1 = Wave 2, P2 = forward reserve), this item is registered as P1, matching the review's ruling "optional; if it does not make the app it goes to P1" (see backlog.html ☆6) — it is a Wave 2 item scheduled at §11.6 2c; if 2c slips it is deferred, never downgraded to P2 reserve.
FR-12.7Fairness evidence (cohort parity, embedded in every design-time proposal)P1New

A deterministic engine gives the same output for the same input, so unfairness can only enter at its upstream/downstream edges — recognition error (a group misclassified on intent/identity), step-up dropout (a group that cannot pass secondary verification), and L2 approver variance. Therefore FR-12.1 nominations and FR-12.3 diffs must carry cohort impact analysis; fairness is not solved by "a more neutral AI" but by measuring these three edges and putting them on the signing table.

Acceptance criteria

  • AC1: three gaps monitored — approval-rate gap, TTR gap, step-up completion/dropout gap; proxy dimensions limited to five: language, 62+ age marker, LMI (low-moderate-income census tract), channel, and expressly stated vulnerability markers; gaps < 2pp are a necessary condition for nominations/policy effect (threshold is an assumption, pilot-calibrated).
  • AC2: protected attributes (race, sex, religion, etc.) are not collected (Reg B limits collection itself); proxy variables are used only inside the compliance-analysis environment and never appear in customer views, agent views, or case records.
  • AC3: evidence cards show both "post-rule" and "current human" cohort gaps; cohorts with insufficient sample are explicitly marked "insufficient sample, no conclusion" — fairness claims on small samples are prohibited.
  • AC4: locked-segment translations must be pre-approved (FR-5.1) before generated text may follow customer language; an over-threshold language-dimension gap blocks the corresponding nomination.
Depends on: FR-12.1, FR-12.3, §4.2 parity guardrails; compliance basis: ECOA / Reg B / Fair Lending, UDAAP. Non-goals: no individual profiling, no differentiated-preference strategies, no external fairness scores.

FCR (First Contact Resolution)Share of issues resolved on the customer's very first contact, with no follow-up needed.

CCaaSContact Center as a Service — cloud contact-center platforms (like this product's home platform).

Automation FlowsDeterministic flow orchestration: walking tasks across systems per configured steps, holding state, timers, and approval nodes.
Agent BuilderThe tool for admins to configure AI employees: importing SOPs, generating guardrails, pre-launch validation.
Ticket / CaseThe work unit for a customer letter inside the system; this product's thesis is "email thread is the case".
Universal queueAll channels' mail/calls queue and route uniformly by skill.
Secure messageIn-app inbox messages after online-banking/app login, natively authenticated; distinct from forgeable ordinary email.
Step-up authenticationTemporary extra identity verification before sensitive actions (single-use links, OTPs, online-banking login).
SPF / DKIM / DMARCThe email-domain anti-forgery trio: proving "this letter truly came from that domain" — never proving the sender is the account holder.
Reg E (12 CFR 1005.11)The CFPB-administered Regulation E (EFTA implementing rule) error-resolution terms: customer oral/written error notices start the investigation clock (normally complete investigation within 10 business days, otherwise provisionally credit within 10 business days, 20 for new accounts; investigation up to 45 days, 90 for new-account / POS / foreign transactions); email = written notice; waiting on customer materials never pauses the clock.v0.2 Revised
Reg Z (12 CFR 1026.13)Credit-card billing-dispute rules: written notice within 60 days, acknowledgment within 30 days, resolution within 2 billing cycles (≤90 days).
Temporary credit / Provisional creditMoney advanced to the customer before investigation completes; lawfully reversible if no error is found.
RepresentmentThe stage in a dispute investigation where the merchant submits evidence that "this transaction was fine".
Dispute / ChargebackThe full flow of a customer challenging a transaction.
UDAAPThe ban on unfair, deceptive, or abusive acts/practices: the regulatory red line for customer-facing wording and conduct.
SAR / tipping-offSAR = Suspicious Activity Report; tipping-off = leaking to customers that they were reported/investigated — prohibited by law.
BSA / AMLBank Secrecy Act / anti-money-laundering: the statutory source of bank fraud and suspicious-activity teams' duties.
POA (Power of Attorney)Legal authorization to act on someone else's account; verification permanently human.
BEC / ATOBEC = business email compromise (impersonating identities to steal transfers/info changes); ATO = account takeover (operating after stealing credentials).

SLAService timing commitments; Saga = the failure-compensation pattern for cross-system writes (not database rollback).

FTEFull-time-equivalent headcount; ROI modeling measures labor savings in "FTEs released".
Core bankingCore banking systems (e.g. Fiserv/FIS/Jack Henry): the books-of-record source for accounts, transactions, and fees; digital-banking platforms (Q2/Candescent/Alkami) are the online-banking/app layer.
Runtime / Design-time (v0.3)Runtime = how each inbound email travels the 9 steps (must be deterministic; the LLM sits on no decision path); design-time = who writes rules, nominates delegation, discovers new intents, sees population anomalies (AI's wow space). Design-time AI outputs configuration diffs, not decisions.
Wave (v0.3)A cluster of many differently-worded emails in a time window sharing one root cause: P0 negative waves (e.g. mass card freezes on one BIN range), P1 positive waves (bank errors overcharging, including silent victims who never wrote — enabling mass remediation), P2 friction waves (e.g. login confusion after a release). Thread is the case; a wave is a case of cases.
Ratchet (v0.3)The asymmetric rule for delegation changes: tightening (e.g. L3→L2) may be immediate and must carry auto-expiry; loosening (e.g. L2→L3) must go through backtest → shadow → dual sign. The only automatic direction is downgrade.
Preventable inbound (v0.3)Mail an event-triggered notice could have prevented (card already shipped, customer still asks where it is). AI discovers what was preventable and which notification rule to build; sending itself is a deterministic Automation Flow under locked templates, frequency caps, and opt-out.
Cohort parity (v0.3)Gaps in approval rate / TTR / step-up dropout across five proxy dimensions (language, 62+, LMI census tract, channel, stated vulnerability); < 2pp is a necessary condition for nominations and policy diffs. Protected attributes like race are not collected (Reg B limits collection).
Canary nomination (v0.3)A nomination known to deserve rejection, periodically inserted to test whether signers are still paying attention; a mistaken approval suspends that signer's bulk-signing privilege. Guards against rubber-stamping signed diffs.
PolicyPack diff (v0.3)The Policy Compiler's output: a clause-level config change with human-readable clause and machine-readable rule side by side, plus backtest flips, stale-template list, dissent cards, and cohort impact. Humans sign this diff, not the natural-language sentence.
Remediation (v0.3)Correcting a bank error (e.g. duplicate NSF fees) proactively for all affected customers, including silent ones who never complained. Requires bank-error confirmation, dual sign, staged batches, idempotent and reversible operations.
