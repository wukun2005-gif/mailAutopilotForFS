06 Risk

Top risks would end this product.

RISK 01
Missing a regulated intent
Half a sentence is a running clock. 74% of 2025's 136 Reg E violations sat in error processing.
What we built
  • High-recall detection at ingest, before an agent opens the mail
  • False positives preferred — recall ≥99.5% gates all autonomy
  • No samples in 90 days means no L3
RISK 02
The email attack surface
BEC, ATO and attachment injection — under ATO every normal identity signal still passes.
What we built
  • Injected text is quarantined as data, never as instruction
  • R3 combinations have no callable function on this channel
  • Confirmation goes only to on-file contacts
RISK 03
Numeric hallucination
An invented fee or date is a Reg E and UDAAP violation; one wrong write can be unrecoverable.
What we built
  • Amounts, dates and deadlines come only from system slots — the model never writes a number
  • R4 is pinned to L0; credentials sit at the tool layer
  • The matrix beats the model

Also on the register: tipping-off vs duty to speak (31 CFR 1020.320(e)) · over-disclosure to an unverified sender (GLBA) · third-party risk review · vulnerable-customer fairness · the judge itself failing (14% verdict flip rate). Full list in Backup B10.

Backup · B4 Questions I expect

Six questions, short answers.

“CXA Ops Center and Agent Builder already exist — what is new?”
The platform is the base, explicitly labelled as reused. New is the FS email vertical: regulated-intent detection at ingestion, assurance-tiered delegation with in-thread step-up, the dispute-intake agent the catalogue does not have, email attack-surface defence, and a compliance-signable dossier.
“Glia already guarantees no hallucination and no injection impact.”
They govern what the AI says, topic by topic, and the guarantee covers Strict mode. We govern what it says, does and to whom — segment-level control inside one reply, plus an action-risk × identity-assurance matrix that governs writes, not wording.
“Is 25–35% VARR realistic?”
It is labelled an assumption. The anchor is Talkdesk's own 2026 finding: 38% of leading organisations already resolve over 40% of contacts — all-channel, not email. Step one of the pilot backtests on the customer's own 90 days of mail.
“How do you establish identity over email at all?”
We do not pretend to. SPF, DKIM and DMARC prove the domain was not forged, not that the sender holds the account — so identity is a four-level assurance rating recomputed on every arrival, with a single-use in-thread step-up link.
“Why not go after the big banks first?”
They self-build and Sierra owns that narrative. Mid-size banks and credit unions buy off the shelf, keep public mailboxes, sit under Reg E, and cluster exactly where Talkdesk's install base and core integrations already are — 262 institutions.
“Cannibalising seats — does Talkdesk lose?”
On assumption-based math: 32 seats × Elite $165/seat/month × 12 ≈ $63k of licence a year — platform revenue, not bank cost. The same volume at $1.50 per resolution earns ~$268k. Net-additive for us, ~$2.68M a year for the bank. If we do not build it, that revenue goes to Glia, Sierra or Salesforce.

Backup · B6 Trade-offs

Reversible earns it. Irreversible never does.

We choose
The price we accept
Default L2, graduate one intent at a time
Every write starts behind a human one-click and only moves to L3 with backtest evidence and a dual signature.
↔
A slower first month
We ship fewer autonomous actions than a rival promising 90% on day one — and compliance signs the same sheet as the business.
The harder denominator
VARR counts every valid inbound email, including disputes, fraud reports and multi-intent mail.
↔
A lower published number
35% of the eligible pool reads as ~30% of all intake — we report both, side by side, so no one can call it denominator-picking.
Dispute intake only — never adjudication
AI files, clocks, receipts and chases; investigators decide, AI drafts the outcome letter, a person signs it.
↔
The biggest line item on the table
We give up the full-dispute automation prize and keep the licence to operate — R4 cells have no graduation path at all.

Also given up, on purpose: assembled letters over fluent ones, in-app step-up over a link, a runtime of our own, and mega-bank deals — they build, and Sierra owns that story.

Hard “never” list · no graduation path
  • Adjudicating disputes or issuing provisional credit
  • Credit, underwriting, claim denial, above-threshold fee waivers
  • Investment or insurance advice, legal-document responses
  • R3 writes: contact info, address, reissue to a new address, paying third parties
  • POA, bereavement, identity-relationship changes
  • Any confirmatory reply to a suspected-fraud or compromised account
  • Disclosing full account numbers or credentials to an unverified party
  • Any silent skip of a Reg E or Reg Z clock node
  • Unlocking cards, unfreezing, clearing fraud flags
  • Requesting passwords, full card numbers, CVV or OTP over email
  • Requiring a signed statement, police report or “contact the merchant first” before accepting a dispute

Sort order: volume × determinability × identity achievability, then gated by regulatory risk. Wave is delivery order, not importance. The configuration layer must not offer a switch for anything on the “never” list — they are not off by default, they are absent.
